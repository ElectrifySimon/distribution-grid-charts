Distribution grids, read from the source PDFs

Sixteen charts on the grid at the end of the line

Two reading methods, stated on every card. PDFs go through vector geometry: axes calibrated from the printed tick labels, series taken from the printed legend, values sampled at each tick. Interactive charts publish their own data table, which is read directly and then checked against a number the publisher prints in the chart headline. Nothing is estimated by eye. Charts that failed either test are described at the bottom rather than shown.

Why the list is short

Fifteen reports from eleven organisations were downloaded and every figure in them put through the reader: IEA Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions and World Energy Outlook 2026, Eurelectric Grids for Speed, ACER's 2024 infrastructure monitoring report, ACER-CEER guidance on distribution network planning, EU DSO Entity annual reporting, IRENA World Energy Transitions Outlook 2023, three EPRI distribution publications, Ember's grids deck, the Green Grids Initiative finance principles, ISGAN's 2023 annual report and two ETC grids documents. 288 chart panels were detected.

A panel is published only when its legend labels appear verbatim in the page's text layer, no two series share a name, and the values pass an internal test such as shares summing to 100 or a category total matching the figure the report states in prose.

Ember needed a different route. Its charts are published as interactive embeds, so I enumerated its site and found 74 grid charts, then pulled the data table each one ships. 31 returned a usable table. Each was checked against the number printed in its own headline, its segment totals or its parts: 13 passed. Five are about distribution networks and are shown here. Of the rest, four concern transmission, one has unlabelled periods, and three are about generation mix or prices. Every verdict, including the eighteen that failed their own arithmetic or offered no number to check against, is in audit.json.

The failures cluster into three causes. EPRI, Ember, GGI and ISGAN ship their charts as raster images inside layout-heavy documents, so there is no vector geometry to calibrate. ETC's reports sit behind an email download form, and its public grids PDFs are slide exports whose axis words read as data. IEA, ACER and IRENA use multi-panel composites that share one legend across panels, or legends with repeated column headers, which the reader pairs wrongly. Those are reader limits, not publisher errors, and the first of them needs an image reader rather than a better vector parser.
